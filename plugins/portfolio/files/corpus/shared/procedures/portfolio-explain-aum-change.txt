Explaining a quarter-over-quarter AUM change to a client

Section 1: Gather the Figures
1. Retrieve the account's or household's quarter-end AUM for the two quarter ends being compared.
2. Calculate the total change in US dollars and as a percentage.
3. List all external cash flows (contributions and withdrawals) that settled between the two quarter-end dates, including transfers to or from other household accounts.
4. Check the valuation history for any correction to either quarter-end value.

Section 2: Break Down the Change
1. Net flows: add contributions and subtract withdrawals settled in the quarter.
2. Corrections: note any change caused by a corrected price or a restated valuation.
3. Market movement: the remainder after flows and corrections is investment result (price change plus income, less fees debited from the account).
4. Confirm the quarter's time-weighted return is consistent with the market movement component.
   A large difference usually means a flow was missed or dated in the wrong quarter.

Section 3: Explain to the Client
1. Present the change as three lines: flows, market movement, corrections.
2. Explain that rebalancing trades during the quarter moved money between asset classes but did not change total value.
3. If the client asks why value rose while return was negative (or the reverse), point to the flows.
4. If held-away assets are shown in the household view, clarify whether they are part of the figure being discussed.

Section 4: Refer Fee Questions to the Billing Desk
1. If the client asks how the AUM change affects the fee, refer them to the billing desk.
   The quarter-end AUM is the billable starting point, but exclusions, household aggregation and fee band boundaries are applied by billing.
2. Refer immediately if the AUM crossed a fee band boundary, if a correction was made after billing ran, or if the client disputes an invoice.
3. Record the explanation and any referral in the household notes.
