Rebalancing an account that has drifted outside tolerance

Section 1: Confirm the Drift
1. Open the drift report for the account and note the valuation date it was measured on.
2. Identify every asset class whose absolute drift exceeds the model's drift tolerance, with its direction (overweight or underweight) and size in percentage points.
3. Confirm the valuation date is current and that no price on the pricing exception list affects the account.
   If a price is under review, wait for it to clear before trading; a bad price can create false drift.
4. Check for pending contributions, withdrawals or an account-level cash reserve that explains the drift.
   New cash waiting to be invested is usually resolved by buying underweight asset classes, not by a full rebalance.

Section 2: Generate the Proposal
1. Start a rebalance proposal from the account's current holdings against its assigned model portfolio.
2. For taxable accounts, confirm the tax-aware settings (loss harvesting, short-term gain avoidance, wash-sale window).
3. Review the proposed trades and the estimated drift after trading for every asset class.
   Every asset class should end inside its tolerance band; if tax settings leave one outside, note the reason.
4. Confirm the proposal preserves the model's cash target and any cash buffer for upcoming withdrawals.

Section 3: Approve and Execute
1. Submit the proposal for approval by a user with trading permission; obtain a second approval if the firm requires it for the account type or trade size.
2. Send approved orders to the custodian before the trading cutoff.
3. Proposals not approved within five business days expire; regenerate them from current prices rather than reusing them.

Section 4: Verify
1. On the next valuation cycle, rerun the drift check and confirm the account is inside tolerance.
2. Record the rebalance date and reason in the account notes.
3. Remember that the rebalance changes weights, not total market value; it does not by itself change the account's quarter-end AUM.
