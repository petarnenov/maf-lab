# Household Grouping at Acme Wealth Partners

## Grouping Principles

Acme Wealth Partners aggregates accounts into households so that clients receive the benefit of tiered pricing across all their related assets. A household normally includes accounts owned by spouses or domestic partners, their dependent children, and trusts or entities where they are the primary beneficiaries. Accounts held for unrelated individuals must never be grouped, even at a client's request, because doing so would misstate the fee each client pays.

## Special Cases

### Business Accounts

Accounts owned by an operating business controlled by the household may be grouped only if the advisor documents the ownership in the household notes and a FIRM_ADMIN approves the grouping. Institutional plans are always billed as their own household.

### Split Households

When a household separates, OPS splits the accounts into two households effective on the next billing period start. The advisor must confirm which schedule each new household uses; if the new household is missing a schedule, the next run will fail with FS-REQUIRED.

## Impact on Approval

Because the $25,000 approval threshold is applied to the household invoice, grouping additional accounts can push an invoice over the threshold. Advisors should expect FIRM_ADMIN review when large accounts are added to an existing household near quarter end.
