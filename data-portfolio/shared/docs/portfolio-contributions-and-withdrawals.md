# Contributions and Withdrawals

## External Cash Flows

A contribution is money or securities that the client moves into a managed account from outside; a withdrawal is money or securities moved out to the client or to a third party. Together they are called external cash flows. Transfers between two accounts in the same household are recorded as a withdrawal from one account and a contribution to the other, so the household total is unaffected while each account's value changes. Dividends, interest and internal trades are not external flows; they are investment activity that stays inside the account.

## Effect on Market Value and AUM

External flows change an account's total market value directly. A $250,000 contribution raises the account's value by $250,000 on the day it settles, before any market movement, and a withdrawal lowers it by the amount paid out. Because quarter-end AUM is the account's total market value on the last business day of the quarter, a large contribution in the final weeks of a quarter can lift quarter-end AUM noticeably even if markets were flat. When explaining a change in AUM between two quarter ends, the change is split into three parts: net external flows, market movement, and any valuation corrections.

## Effect on Drift and Rebalancing

A contribution arrives as cash and immediately raises the account's cash weight, often pushing cash outside its tolerance band. Firms usually invest new money on the next threshold check by buying the underweight asset classes, which moves the account back toward its model without selling anything. A withdrawal is funded first from cash above the model's cash target and then by selling the most overweight asset classes, so a well-timed withdrawal can reduce drift rather than increase it.

## Effect on Performance

Time-weighted return (TWR) removes the effect of external cash flows so that performance reflects only investment decisions. The period is broken at each flow date, the return of each sub-period is measured on the value actually invested, and the sub-period returns are chained together. A client who contributes $1 million does not see a jump in TWR, because that money was not earned. Money-weighted return, by contrast, does reflect the size and timing of flows, and is available for clients who want to see their personal experience.

## Timing Relative to Quarter End

A flow is included in quarter-end value if it settles on or before the last business day of the quarter. Flows initiated but not settled by quarter end appear in the following quarter. Wires received after the custodian's cutoff on the final day are recorded on the next business day. Advisors expecting a large contribution or distribution near quarter end should note it on the account so that the quarter-end AUM change can be explained without investigation.
