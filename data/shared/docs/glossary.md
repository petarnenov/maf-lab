# Billing Glossary

## Asset and Account Terms

### AUM and Household

**AUM.** Assets under management (AUM) is the market value of the billable holdings in an account at the valuation date. Billable AUM may exclude certain positions, such as held-away assets, cash sweep balances when a firm chooses to exclude cash, or securities flagged as unsupervised. AUM is captured from the custodian feed and priced by the platform's valuation service, and the same figure is used for both breakpoint evaluation and fee calculation.

**Household.** A household is a group of related accounts, usually belonging to one family, that are billed together. The combined AUM of a household determines its tier or breakpoint rate. Each account belongs to at most one household at a time, and membership changes are effective-dated so that past periods remain reproducible.

## Schedule Terms

### Breakpoint and Proration

**Breakpoint.** A breakpoint is an AUM threshold at which a different fee rate applies. In a breakpoint schedule the new rate applies to the whole balance, while in a tiered schedule it applies only to the portion of the balance above the threshold.

**Proration.** Proration is the adjustment of a fee to reflect only part of a billing period, for example when an account opens or closes mid-period, or when a large contribution or withdrawal occurs and the firm has enabled flow-based proration. Proration uses the firm's day-count convention.

## Timing Terms

### Billing Period and Arrears and Advance

**Billing Period.** A billing period is the span of time a fee covers, typically a calendar month or calendar quarter. Each run is tied to exactly one billing period, defined by its period start and period end dates.

**Arrears and Advance.** Billing in arrears charges for a period after it ends, using AUM at the end of the period or an average over it. Billing in advance charges at the start of a period for the coming months, using AUM at the start. Advance billing requires refunds or prorated credits when an account closes early, while arrears billing requires a closing fee when an account leaves mid-period.
